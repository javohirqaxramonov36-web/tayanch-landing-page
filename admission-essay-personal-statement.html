Personal Statement qanday yoziladi

Common App insho — sizning ovozingiz, tajribangiz va fikrlash tarzingiz ko'rinadigan yagona qism. Bu sahifa uni tuzish, yozish va sayqallashning amaliy yo'l-yo'rig'i.

Nega bu insho boshqacha

Baholovchi komissiya sizning baholaringizni allaqachon ko'rgan. Ular bu insho orqali boshqa hech qayerda ko'rinmaydigan narsani — qanday fikrlashingizni — izlaydi.

Transkript va bal ko'rsatkichlari sizning nima qilganingizni ko'rsatadi. Personal statement esa nega va qanday qilganingizni ko'rsatadi — qaysi qadriyatlar, kuzatishlar yoki tajribalar sizni hozirgi holatingizga olib kelgan.

Komissiya a'zosi bir kunda o'nlab insholarni o'qiydi. Ular esda qoladigan narsa — mavzuning katta-kichikligi emas, balki aniq bir tafsilotning haqiqiyligi.

Bu rezyume emas. Yutuqlar ro'yxatini qayta yozish — eng ko'p uchraydigan yo'qotilgan imkoniyat. Komissiya sizning yutuqlaringizni boshqa bo'limlardan allaqachon biladi.

Bu shuningdek "qahramonlik hikoyasi" ham emas. Insho — o'sish jarayoni haqida: nimani anglab yetdingiz, qanday o'zgardingiz, bugun nimani boshqacha ko'rasiz.

Odatda bitta insho 3–8 daqiqada o'qiladi. Komissiya uchta savolga javob izlaydi:

  • Bu talaba o'zi haqida qanday fikrlaydi?
  • U qiyinchilik yoki tanlov oldida qanday harakat qiladi?
  • Kampusga kelganda u nimani olib keladi?

Insho qurish yondashuvlari

Bitta "to'g'ri" shakl yo'q. Tanlov — hikoya qilmoqchi bo'lgan tajribangizning tabiatiga bog'liq.

Narrativ (voqea asosida)

Bitta aniq lahza yoki kunni batafsil tasvirlaysiz, so'ng undan chiqargan xulosangizga o'tasiz. Kichik voqea, katta ma'no.

Montaj (bir nechta lavha)

Bir-biriga bog'liq bo'lmagandek tuyulgan 3–5 ta qisqa lavhani bitta ip — takrorlanuvchi g'oya yoki obraz — orqali bog'laysiz.

Muammo–refleksiya

Real qiyinchilik yoki ziddiyatdan boshlaysiz, uni qanday boshqarganingizni, undan nimani o'rganganingizni ko'rsatasiz.

Kuzatuv–tahlil

Kundalik narsadan (hobbi, oddiy odat, kichik detal) boshlab, u orqali dunyoqarashingizni ochib berasiz.

O'zingizni tekshiring
Talaba oshxonada har yakshanba buvisi bilan taom tayyorlashning uchta turli xotirasini tasvirlab, ularni "sabr" mavzusi bilan bog'lamoqchi. Bu qaysi yondashuvga yaqinroq?

Ichki struktura

Yondashuvdan qat'i nazar, kuchli insholarning aksariyati shu to'rt bosqichdan o'tadi.

1

Kirish lahzasi (hook)

Umumiy jumla bilan emas, o'rtadan — harakat, dialog yoki aniq detal bilan boshlang. O'quvchini darhol sahnaga olib kiring, unga sahnani tushuntirmang.

2

Zamin va kontekst

Nima sodir bo'lganini, nega bu vaziyat siz uchun muhim bo'lganini qisqa tushuntiring — lekin bu qism hikoyaning eng qisqa qismi bo'lsin.

3

Burilish nuqtasi

Fikringiz yoki qarashingiz o'zgargan aniq lahza. Bu insho markazi — vaqt eng ko'p shu yerga sarflansin.

4

Refleksiya va bugungi kun

O'sha tajriba sizni bugun qanday odam qilganini ko'rsating. Kelajakka qarab tugating — nimani izlayotganingiz, nimani olib kelayotganingiz.

O'zingizni tekshiring
Insho vaqtining eng katta qismi qaysi bosqichga ajratilishi kerak?

Til va ovoz

Bir xil voqeani ikki talaba butunlay boshqacha eshittirishi mumkin — farq so'z tanlovida.

"Men mehnatkash edim" deyish o'rniga, mehnatkashlikni ko'rsatadigan lahzani tasvirlang — soat nechada uyg'onganingiz, nimani qurbon qilganingiz. O'quvchi xulosani o'zi chiqarsin.

"Ko'p narsani o'rgandim" — hech narsa aytmaydi. "Elektr sxemasidagi bitta noto'g'ri ulangan rezistorni topish uchun uch soat sarfladim" — sizni boshqa hech kimga o'xshamaydigan qiladi.

Lug'atdan topilgan "murakkab" so'zlar emas, o'zingiz kundalik gapirganday jumlalar tuzing. Baholovchi ming nafar 17 yoshli bilan gaplashgan — sun'iy ohangni darhol sezadi.

O'zingizni tekshiring
Quyidagilardan qaysi biri "ko'rsatish" (show), "aytish" (tell) emas?

Tez-tez uchraydigan xatolar

Bu xatolarning aksariyati insho "yomon" bo'lgani uchun emas, balki muharrirlik bosqichida yetarlicha qayta ko'rilmagani uchun yuzaga keladi.

—

Juda ko'p voqeani qamrab olishga urinish

Butun yozgi tatilni yoki bir necha yillik tajribani bir insho ichiga sig'dirish — natijada hech qaysi lahza chuqur ochilmay qoladi. Bitta tor mavzuni tanlang, uni chuqur yoriting.

—

Kirish qismida uzoq sarson bo'lish

Birinchi ikki-uch jumla o'quvchini ushlab qolishi kerak. Umumiy falsafiy gap bilan ("Hayot — bu safar...") boshlash e'tiborni zaiflashtiradi.

—

Refleksiyasiz voqea

Faqat "nima bo'lgani"ni tasvirlab, "bu meni qanday o'zgartirgani"ni yozmaslik — komissiyaga eng ko'p yetishmayotgan qism aynan shu.

—

Boshqa birovning ovozi bilan yozish

Haddan tashqari "silliqlangan", so'zma-so'z tahrirlangan matn haqiqiylikni yo'qotadi. Baholovchi asl ovozni his qilishi kerak.

—

Salbiy yakunlash

Qiyinchilikni tasvirlab, undan qanday chiqib ketganingizni ko'rsatmasdan tugatish — insho tugagach o'quvchida "va nima bo'ldi?" degan savol qolmasin.

Asosiy Common App Insho

When I noticed a small piece of paper on the kitchen table, I thought it was just a bill. It was a notice for a large bank debt. My father had been carrying it alone — working long hours, never mentioning it so we wouldn't worry. So I decided to act.

After financial difficulties struck my family in tenth grade, I quit my IELTS prep course and prepared alone — old, free Telegram materials, one practice test a day. That August, we sold timber trees from our yard to cover the registration fee. I scored a 7.0, then used the same method for the SAT, hitting 1420. When a neighbor asked me to tutor her son that September, word spread within days, and two weeks later I was teaching at Sydney School. Watching a student go from failing to passing felt more rewarding than any score of mine — I mentored around 40 of them before leaving early.

My interest in civil engineering started with construction, not in a classroom — it started in our kitchen. From sixteen, I worked alongside my father on construction tasks — pouring concrete, sanding walls, laying flooring. One afternoon, while redoing our kitchen, I measured a section of tiling, confident I'd gotten it right. My father checked before we cut — and he was right: the measurements didn't match, and cutting first would've wasted tile we couldn't replace. He didn't scold me; he simply showed me how to re-measure, twice, before cutting once. That day I learned something no textbook teaches: what feels right isn't always right.

To handle the heavy load of school, work, and teaching without losing focus, I had to build strict routines. Between school, exams, and teaching, I filled ten journals — tracking mistakes, setting routines, emptying my head onto paper. When that wasn't enough, boxing — arriving drained, leaving exhausted — cleared what willpower alone couldn't. Most of what I wrote, I followed through on.

After graduating, I worked long hours of manual labor. That summer, I started learning Calculus I, AutoCAD, and Prompt Engineering, and realized how important BIM, Revit, and Digital Twin technology were — they could turn my father's instinct into real planning. The first time I modeled our kitchen in Revit, my father looked at the screen, then pointed at the wall we'd almost cut into years earlier. Using AI reduced my drafting time from two hours to fifteen minutes — time I spent checking load paths and budgets, the same judgment my father used on that kitchen floor, now visible on a screen.

That habit didn't stay in our kitchen. In 2023, I spent weekends fixing potholes on a rural road near us — clearing debris and filling gaps left unfixed for years. It was the same lesson at a different scale: a road no one measured twice becomes a road 100 villagers no longer trust. I started noticing this everywhere — cracked walls, flooded streets after every spring rain, and buildings that skipped a step someone should've caught.

My long-term goal is to build the Engineering Academy — a scholarship-funded STEM program for students who have the ability but not the opportunity. Uzbekistan is building faster than it's training engineers to check the work. What's missing isn't ambition — it's opportunity. An academy built on that principle would put 'measure twice' into the hands of students who'd otherwise never get near a blueprint — and turn today's uncounted potential into the generation that builds Uzbekistan's roads and cities to last. It's the same thing my father gave me, just at a bigger scale.

A few years ago, a small piece of paper on our kitchen table filled me with helplessness. Today I look at a blank screen and see a blueprint — for a wall, a road, or an academy — and I know exactly how to measure it before I cut.

Gap Year Tushuntirish Inshosi

When I graduated in May 2025, my family still had financial constraints. So, I spent several months of my gap year doing heavy manual labor — loading and unloading cargo on back-to-back day and night shifts, at times sleeping only two hours after a hard 22-hour shift. This covered my expenses and supported my family's budget.

Once our situation got better after six months, I moved on to my academic goals: learning Building Information Modeling (BIM), Revit, and Digital Twin technology, then adding Calculus I and physics through Khan Academy. I also began experimenting with Prompt Engineering across different AI models, curious whether they could do more than answer questions. Working through the Autodesk Education Plan for a free AutoCAD license, I kept practicing — until, stuck rebuilding the same repetitive drawing detail by hand, I tried getting AI to write the AutoLISP script instead. It worked, and a detail that used to take two hours now took fifteen minutes. I used the same trick to automate other repetitive parts of my drawings.

This gap year was not a pause. It began with physically exhausting work that left little room for anything else, and is ending with me having built, mostly on my own, the technical foundation I need for civil engineering — while I also prepare to retake IELTS in September, since my current certificate expires in August. Both parts taught me the same lesson: Discipline isn't about feeling motivated — it's about showing up anyway, even when you don't know if it'll work out.

Ikkala qism ham bir xulosa beradi: intizom motivatsiyaga bog'liq emas.

Maktab Almashtirish Inshosi

In 2022, I sat for the competitive entrance exam of a newly opened specialized school for mathematics, physics, and foreign languages in the Urta Chirchik district, and I was accepted.

The shift was immediate. There were more exams, and they were harder. The school moved most assessments onto strict, computer-based testing, and financial strain at home meant missing classes some weeks, then learning it myself afterward. I adapted, but the adjustment I remember best happened in physics class.

Until then, I had only ever learned from books, notes, and recorded video lessons — information only ever moved one way — into me. Then my physics teacher, Mrs. Mukhlisa, opened a simulation on the classroom's electronic board: a projectile, and a slider for the launch angle. Students came up, dragged the angle, and watched the arc change in real time — not a video of physics, but physics responding to a touch.

I sat there struck by how obvious it suddenly seemed: understanding wasn't only something you learned by reading a page over and over. Sometimes it was something you could touch and change until it made sense.

But it was Mrs. Mukhlisa's board, more than any exam, that changed how I look for solutions today: before assuming a problem is hard, I first ask whether I am simply looking at it the wrong way.
